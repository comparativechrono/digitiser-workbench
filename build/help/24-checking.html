Checking a digitisation, and putting it right

A digitisation is right when what is drawn in pink lies on what the figure shows, and the scales rest on the right ticks. For the examples, Compare with the known values says how close it is; for your own figures, the overlay is the check.

  1. The scales. Are the green tick values on the axes the figure's own? A label misread (a 6 for an 8) is usually outvoted by the others; if not, the Check list says the confidence is medium or low – mark two ticks yourself.
  2. The plot areas. Does each green box sit on the axis lines?
  3. What was found. Zoom in along the pink: does it follow the line, sit on each marker, reach the end of each bar?
  4. Spot checks. Put the mouse on a few points: the status bar's value should be what you read off the axes by eye.
What you seeWhat to do
A legend, a label or an inset read as dataLeave it out (Leave out).
The wrong line, or several where you want onePick its colour (Colour).
A black or grey line not read (or the axes read as one)Black or grey lines: always (or never).
An axis "not calibrated", or read wronglyTwo ticks and their values (Tick), or the values at the plot's edges (Axes).
A panel missed, or one too manyMark the plot areas (Plot area).
Points missed or found where there are noneMarker size or Colour tolerance; then Edit points.
A scan whose values drift across the plotStraighten it first.
A line whose values look like stepsThe picture is coarse: a sharper copy (a higher resolution from the PDF).
